## Windshield Replacement in Victorville, CA

A guy drove into a shop last month in a 2022 Accord. A ladder had fallen off a truck on the I-15 and gone straight through his windshield. Glass everywhere, and he was rattled.

He asked how long a fix would take. Three hours, maybe four if the cameras needed recalibrating. He looked confused. "Cameras?"

Turned out his car had a camera mounted behind the windshield for the lane-keeping system. Replace the glass, and that camera has to be re-aimed or the safety features stop working right. Windshield replacement isn't just glass anymore. It's technology sitting behind that glass too.

## When Replacement Is the Right Call

Not every crack needs a full replacement. Small chips and thin cracks under 12 inches often qualify for [repair](/services/windshield-crack-repair/) instead, which is cheaper and faster.

**Replacement is the right call if:**

- The crack is longer than 12 inches
- There are multiple cracks
- The damage sits in the driver's direct line of sight
- The crack reaches the edge of the windshield
- The glass is shattered or has a hole

If that's your situation, the whole windshield comes out. It's the only safe option at that point.

## OEM Glass vs. Aftermarket Glass

OEM means Original Equipment Manufacturer, made by the same company that supplied your factory windshield. Same specs, exact match, higher price.

Aftermarket glass comes from a different manufacturer but meets the same safety standards and fits correctly. For most drivers it's the practical choice, cheaper and reliable.

**OEM makes more sense for:**

- Luxury vehicles (BMW, Mercedes, Audi, Lexus)
- Newer cars, under 3 years old
- Cars with cameras, sensors, or heads-up displays

Both options get stocked, and the right call usually comes down to your car and your budget.

## The Replacement Process

1. **Remove the old windshield** — cut through the old adhesive, lift it out carefully
2. **Clean the frame** — old adhesive and debris both have to go, or you get leaks later
3. **Apply new adhesive** — automotive-grade urethane, strong and waterproof
4. **Set the new windshield** — aligned correctly before it's pressed into place
5. **Let it cure** — usually 1-2 hours before the car is safe to drive
6. **Test everything** — leak check, seal check, camera recalibration if needed

Total time runs 2-4 hours. Wait on-site or drop the car off.

## ADAS Recalibration Explained

ADAS stands for Advanced Driver Assistance Systems, the umbrella term for lane departure warning, automatic emergency braking, adaptive cruise control, and forward collision warning.

These systems often rely on a camera mounted right behind the windshield. Swap the glass and that camera can shift out of alignment, even by a small amount. Recalibration re-aims it with specialized equipment so the safety features actually work the way they're supposed to.

Not every car needs this. Older vehicles usually don't have the hardware. Anything made after 2015 has a decent chance of needing it. Recalibration adds roughly an hour and $100-$200 to the job, and it's worth doing right.

## What It Costs

Luxury or specialty vehicles: $800-$1,500

Most comprehensive insurance policies cover replacement, with deductibles typically $100-$500. Claims get processed and paperwork handled directly with the insurer, so the out-of-pocket cost usually lands at just the deductible.

## Can You Drive with a Cracked Windshield?

Technically, sometimes. Legally, it depends on where the crack sits. California law prohibits driving with damage that obstructs the driver's view, and a crack in that zone can mean a fix-it ticket.

There's a safety angle too. Your windshield is structural. It helps airbags deploy correctly and keeps the roof from collapsing in a rollover. A cracked windshield doesn't hold up as well in a crash.
