## Windshield Crack Repair in Victorville, CA

A woman called in last July. It was 110 degrees out. She'd noticed a small crack the day before, just a couple inches. She parked at the mall for two hours in direct sun. When she came back out, the crack ran clean across the whole windshield.

She thought she needed a full replacement. Turned out the crack was long but thin, one clean line, not spiderwebbed. It got repaired that day for about $120.

That's what desert heat does to a windshield crack. It doesn't sit still. It grows.

[Image: Windshield crack spreading in desert sun]

## Why Cracks Spread Fast Here

Glass expands when it heats up and shrinks when it cools. Basic physics, but Victorville pushes it to extremes. Daytime highs over 100°F, sometimes 115°F in peak summer, then a 30-40 degree drop after sunset. Your windshield goes through that swing every single day.

If there's already a crack, that expansion and contraction works it like a lever. Cracks that would sit stable for weeks in a mild climate can grow six inches in one hot afternoon here. That's why waiting is the wrong move in this town, more than almost anywhere else.

## The Dollar Bill Rule

A quick way to check if a crack might be repairable: lay a dollar bill across it. If the crack is shorter than the bill (about 6 inches) and not deep, it's usually a good repair candidate. Longer than that, and replacement gets more likely.

**Cracks we can repair:**

- Under 12 inches long
- Not directly in the driver's line of sight
- Not touching the edge of the windshield
- Not gone through both layers of glass

**Cracks that need replacement instead:**

- Longer than 12 inches
- Right in front of the driver's eyes
- On the edge (this weakens the whole frame)
- Deep or spiderwebbed

If yours doesn't qualify for repair, [windshield replacement](/services/windshield-replacement/) is the honest next step, and we'll tell you straight instead of trying to sell a repair that won't hold.

## How the Repair Works

We clean out the crack first, removing dirt and moisture completely. Then a bridge tool holds a resin injector over the damage. Resin flows into the crack and fills the gaps and fractures.

Once it's cured, sometimes with UV light, sometimes just the Victorville sun doing the work in 20 minutes, the resin bonds permanently to the glass and stops the crack from spreading further.

The whole job takes about an hour, sometimes 90 minutes for a longer crack. You drive away right after. No cure time to sit through.

## Repair or Replace: A Straight Answer

Under 12 inches, outside the driver's sightline? Repair it. Cheaper, faster, done.

Over 12 inches? It won't hold as a repair. Replacement is the only real option.

In the driver's direct line of sight? Even a clean repair leaves some distortion. That's not something you want between your eyes and the road.

Not sure which one you're dealing with? Bring it in and we'll give you a straight answer either way.

## Insurance Usually Covers This

Comprehensive coverage typically includes glass repair, and the deductible is often lower than your regular deductible, sometimes $0. Since the repair itself only runs $100-$150, most people find it's worth filing instead of paying out of pocket.

## What Happens If You Wait

The math is simple. Repair costs $100-$150. A full replacement once the crack spreads runs $300-$600. Waiting doesn't save money, it costs more later.

There's also the legal side. California law prohibits driving with damage in your direct line of sight. A crack that starts small and outside your view can grow into that territory fast in this heat.
